The problem is not only
who we vote for.

We vote for people who carry hundreds of bundled proposals. It becomes almost impossible to know what our vote actually approves.

01 · Bundles

Ideas sold as packages

Agreeing with a candidate on energy does not mean agreeing with their positions on taxation, pensions or immigration.

02 · Objectives

Promises that are hard to measure

A policy should state before it starts what evidence will allow us to conclude that it succeeded or failed.

03 · Accountability

Too little feedback

A law can survive despite poor outcomes. The V-cycle requires measurement, verification and correction.

The V-cycle applied
to politics.

Every decision should be traceable to a problem, an objective and a set of requirements. The path is then followed back upward to verify the outcome.

A few starting
principles.

They are deliberately separated from political parties and can evolve publicly with full traceability.

P01

Vote on ideas

Express choices issue by issue instead of accepting an entire programme as a single package.

P02

Define before acting

The problem, objective, funding and success criteria are established before implementation.

P03

Verify independently

Results should rely on accessible data and a method that can be publicly challenged.

P04

Intergenerational responsibility

One generation should not transfer to future generations the cost of its own present consumption.

P05

Explicit funding

Every spending measure or revenue cut states where the money comes from and who ultimately bears the cost.

P06

Correct or remove

A policy that fails to meet its objectives should not survive through institutional inertia alone.

A policy is not finished when it is passed. It is finished when its outcome has been verified.
